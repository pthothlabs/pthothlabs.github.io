## 👩‍🏫 Mamta Konnuri

### Teaching Counting to the Youngest Learners

*Teacher Intern · Internship Journal | 22 August 2026*

Teaching a Class 1 child to count sounds like the easiest lesson in the world. In practice, it is one of the most delicate, because everything that follows in Maths is built on it.



![Teaching in the classroom](mamta-week6-1.jpg)



In week 6, I worked with children from Classes 1 to 3 on counting and Natural Numbers, using my **Fun Maths Learning** website along with hands-on classroom activities.

#### Seven steps, one lesson

- **Observe:** I started by checking what the children already knew about counting and numbers.
- **Teach:** I explained counting from 1 to 10 using real objects like pencils, toys and fruits.
- **Interact:** I asked simple questions, and the children answered and counted aloud.
- **Involve:** the children counted, picked, grouped and showed objects themselves.
- **Identify:** I looked for who understood counting and who needed more support.
- **Reinforce:** I repeated the tricky parts with simple examples and activities.
- **Recall:** at the end, we quickly revised counting and Natural Numbers.

#### Why real objects matter

For young children, numbers are abstract until they can hold something. A pencil, a toy or a piece of fruit makes "three" something they can see and touch. Counting aloud adds another layer, since they are hearing, saying and doing at the same time.

I also used counting games from Topmarks, which are made for younger children, to keep the lesson playful. When counting feels like a game, children practise without feeling like they are practising.

My approach this week: Observe > Teach > Interact > Involve > Identify > Reinforce > Recall

#### Takeaway

Two small habits made a big difference this week: starting with observation instead of assuming, and ending with a quick recall. Starting tells me where each child is. Ending helps the day's learning stay with them.
