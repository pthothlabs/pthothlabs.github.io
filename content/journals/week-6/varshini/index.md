## 🤝 Varshini

### Learning Through Play and Practice

*Point of Contact (POC) Manager · Internship Report, Weeks 6 & 7*

*A combined report on my sixth and seventh weeks.*

**Focus of the weeks:** Science week · Mathematics week · Games, quizzes, puzzles and simulations

#### 1. Introduction

My sixth and seventh weeks as a Point of Contact (POC) Manager worked as a pair. One Saturday was a Science and General Knowledge day, and the other was a Mathematics day. In both, I stayed friendly with the students and connected with our teachers and researchers, while our researchers observed the classes from Standard 1 to 10. This report brings the two weeks together, organised by class level, so the patterns across the two days are easier to see.

| Science week | Mathematics week | My role |
|---|---|---|
| Plants and animals, pH scale, human heart, a science website and a GK quiz | Fractions, Tangram puzzles, multiplication, factors and division | Encouraging students and carrying their voices to the team |

#### 2. Classes 1 to 3

In the Science week, the youngest class was taught with a seven-step method (Observe, Explain, Explore, Interact, Play, Apply and Recall). The teacher started from the very basics and then moved to a Fun Science Learning website that she built herself. The researcher rated curiosity at about 90%, understanding at about 80% and application and recall at about 78%. Participation, at about 65%, was uneven, but quieter students were gradually joining in, and the class had improved compared with the previous Saturday.

#### 3. Classes 4 to 6

In the Science week, this class studied plants and animals. The first half, taught with YouTube videos in a combined session, did not work well. Students enjoyed it less and answered less than expected. The second half was much better. Students played games, learned to use a mouse and the basics of a keyboard, and asked to study history next. The researcher also tracked creative moments, such as students adding something extra, asking questions back and drawing what they had understood.

In the Mathematics week, the class studied fractions. After a pre-test, the teacher asked "What is a fraction?" and let students answer in their own words. The students then explored the fractions game on their own before the teacher demonstrated it. Most understood well and worked alone, so the team moved on to times tables and subtraction flash cards. The researchers noted that some students were not comfortable working in pairs, and that working individually brought out a healthy wish to win.

#### 4. Classes 7 to 8

In the Science week, the 15 students in this group took part in a General Knowledge session on animals, using National Geographic Kids. The quiz was the highlight, with about 90% of students actively involved, and about 85% improved in the post-test. Some students needed help with long words, and after support about 75% of them began trying independently. The main problem was noise, because two classes shared a room and about 20% of students were distracted. The planned pair activity was dropped because it was getting complicated.

In the Mathematics week, the same group solved Tangram puzzles on laptops. Every student stayed attentive, and pair work was very strong. Stronger students helped their partners, and pairs explained why an arrangement was right or wrong. Teacher help was needed only at some difficult shapes. Because students already knew shapes and angles, both the pre-test and the post-test went very well.

#### 5. Classes 9 and 10

In the Science week, the students first learned the pH scale with the PhET simulation and an acid, base or neutral sorting activity. About 90% understood at first, and about 95% after extra support. For the 10th standard it worked as revision. In the human heart lesson, an animated video interested about 80% of students, but the theory-heavy topic held the 9th standard less. A drawing activity brought extra interest, with 8 of 15 students especially keen. Some students also asked the mentors for help in Maths and English.

In the Mathematics week, the higher classes used the PhET Arithmetic simulation to predict, check and explain answers. About 95% showed interest in maths. Around 90% solved multiplication correctly and explained their reasoning, and the two students who struggled caught up after using the simulation. About 80% already knew division, and the rest needed more explanation. In the division activity, 14 of 15 students took part.

#### 6. Patterns Across the Two Weeks

Reading the science and mathematics reports side by side, a few patterns appeared in almost every class.

- **Play and interaction beat passive watching.** Games, quizzes, puzzles and simulations kept students attentive, while videos alone worked poorly for the younger classes.
- **Explore first, explain next.** Letting students try a game or simulation before the explanation built curiosity and confidence.
- **Pair work depends on the activity.** It was very strong in the Tangram puzzles, but some students were uncomfortable in pairs during fractions, and a pair activity was dropped in the GK session.
- **Environment matters.** Shared rooms, noise and a smart board that was not working affected attention and planning.

#### 7. My Role as POC Manager

In both weeks my role stayed the same at heart: be friendly, be approachable and keep people connected. I tried to keep students comfortable enough to ask, answer and try things out. With the teachers and researchers, I stayed in touch so everyone knew how each class was going.

What stood out most was how clearly students told us what they want: more games, history topics, extra Maths and English, and sometimes the freedom to work alone. Carrying these voices to the teachers and the MODs is part of the bridge role I have been growing into, so planning can reflect what the students actually ask for.

#### 8. Key Takeaways

| Theme | What these two weeks showed me |
|---|---|
| **Play works** | Games, quizzes, puzzles and simulations produced the highest engagement in both weeks. |
| **Explore before explaining** | Trying first built confidence and curiosity, and understanding followed. |
| **Simulations support beginners** | Students with little prior knowledge caught up after using PhET. |
| **Grouping needs flexibility** | Pairs, groups and individual work each suited different tasks and students. |
| **Students have their own interests** | They asked for history, Maths, English and more time on laptops. |

#### 9. Looking Ahead

Based on both weeks, here is what I would like our team to focus on:

- Teach through games, quizzes, puzzles and simulations instead of relying on videos.
- Let students explore first, and check prior knowledge with a short pre-test.
- Choose pair, group or individual work to suit the task and the students' comfort.
- Hold separate sessions for each class, reduce noise in shared rooms and arrange laptops.
- Include topics students asked for, such as history, Maths and English.

#### 10. Conclusion

Looking at Weeks 6 and 7 together, the strongest lesson is that students learn most when they can play, explore and discuss, and when they feel comfortable doing it. From a self-made science website to Tangram puzzles and PhET simulations, the best moments were interactive ones. I also learned that listening to students is as important as planning the lesson, and that is something I want to keep doing as a POC Manager.

> "When learning feels like play, curiosity does the rest."
