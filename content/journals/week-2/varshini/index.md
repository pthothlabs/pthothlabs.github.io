## 🤝 Varshini Gudigar

### From Hesitation to Friendship

*My second and third weeks as a Point of Contact (POC) Manager · POC Manager*

#### Week 2: When the Silence Started to Break

After the first week of introductions and games, I wasn't sure how much would change. What I noticed in the second week was a real shift. The students who had once stayed quiet started talking to us confidently, and they began asking their own questions.

The difference from Week 1 was clear. The students weren't only interactive during lessons. Outside the classroom too, they started to see us less as outsiders and more as friends. For me, that was the best result of the first two weeks. Students open up to people they trust, and trust had started to grow.

#### Being the Bridge

My role as POC Manager also became clearer in Week 2. Our team worked together well, and I acted as a bridge between our team and the MODs. That meant passing information accurately in both directions, keeping everyone informed, and making sure our work stayed aligned with what the MODs expected.

It taught me that being a POC isn't only about being friendly with students. It also means being dependable for the people you work with. A smooth flow of communication lets the whole team focus on the students instead of on confusion.

#### Week 3: Overcoming the Fear of Being Wrong

In the third week, one thing about the students stood out clearly. The most telling moment was seeing how they approached answering. Many of them already had the answers in their minds and wanted to respond, but they held back because they were scared of getting it wrong. They weren't unsure of the material. They were unsure of being judged. As we kept reminding them that mistakes were welcome, that fear slowly eased, and more hands went up.

> *They weren't unsure of the material. They were unsure of being judged.*

#### What These Weeks Taught Me

Looking at the first three weeks together, a few lessons stand out.

- **Confidence grows when fear of mistakes shrinks.** The students knew more than they thought. They needed a safe space to say it.
- **Friendship makes learning easier.** Students participate more when they feel comfortable with the people teaching them.
- **Coordination matters.** Acting as a bridge showed me how much a team depends on clear communication.

Together, these weeks showed me that the heart of the POC role is trust: between students and our team, and between our team and the MODs. Everything else, from good lessons to smooth coordination, grows from it.
