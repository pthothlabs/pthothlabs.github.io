## 👩‍🏫 Mamta Konnuri

### Week 2: What Happens When You Show Children, Then Step Back

*Teacher Intern · Internship Journal, Week 2*

There is a moment in every lesson that I have started to look forward to. It is the moment after I finish demonstrating something and say, "Now you try."

In week 2, I introduced the students to GCompris, an educational software with a wide range of learning activities. I did not simply open it and let the children loose. I first explained what each activity was about and demonstrated how to do it. Only then did I step back and let them attempt the activities on their own.

#### Why demonstrate first?

When children see how something works, they stop worrying about getting it wrong and start thinking about getting it right. A short demonstration removes a lot of the fear of the unknown, especially for children who are shy or unsure around technology.

#### What I was watching for

While the children worked through the activities, I observed:

- **Understanding level:** did they grasp what the activity was asking?
- **Confidence and participation:** how quickly did they begin?
- **Following instructions:** could they carry out the steps on their own?
- **Problem-solving:** what did they do when they got stuck?
- **Interest and engagement:** were they enjoying it?
- **Need for guidance:** who needed a helping hand, and when?

Some students were quick to start. The more confident ones dived in almost immediately. Others needed a bit of encouragement before they felt ready, and a few needed me nearby to guide them. That is exactly why I never skip the observation part. Watching quietly tells me far more about a child than any question could.

> **My approach this week:** Interact → Observe → Demonstrate → Involve → Independent Attempt → Assess

#### What stays with me

Technology can be a wonderful tool for learning, but it does not teach by itself. It works best when a teacher introduces it with care, shows the way, and then trusts the child to explore. Give children a clear path and the confidence to walk it, and they surprise you.

*Educators, how do you decide when to step in and when to step back during hands-on learning?*
